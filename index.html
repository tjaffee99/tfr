      Where the numbers come from

      Population by single year of age and sex, life tables, the age pattern of childbearing, migration and all historical series are from the UN World Population Prospects 2024, the latest revision (estimates to 2023, projections after).

      Newer figures come from national statistics offices, Eurostat and recent surveys. For 2026, current-year estimates scale the latest official TFR by the change in births reported so far this year — the method used by trackers like Birth Gauge and More Births. Hover any country to see its source.

      How the projection works

      A cohort-component model runs in your browser: single years of age 0–100+, one-year steps from mid-2026 to 2100. Each year people age and die according to the UN's projected life tables, births follow your fertility path applied to the UN's age pattern of childbearing, and migrants arrive by age and sex as the UN assumes.

      Fed the UN's own assumptions it lands within about 1% of the UN medium variant by 2100 for most countries.

      Caveats

      Projections are scenarios, not forecasts. Migration doesn't respond to population pressure. Current-year estimates are based on partial-year births and can move as more months are published. Regions use the UN's regional aggregates; very small territories can deviate more.
