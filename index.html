Fertility Explorer

Total fertility rate (TFR) is the number of children a woman would have at current age-specific birth rates. About 2.1 keeps a population stable without migration. Click a country to see its population pyramid, move the timeline to travel through time, and turn on projections to change future fertility and see what happens.

Data

Population by single year of age and sex, life tables, age-specific fertility, migration and all historical series come from the UN World Population Prospects 2024 (the latest revision; estimates through 2023, projections after). Newer TFRs come from national statistics offices, Eurostat and recent surveys. 2026 values are current-year estimates: the latest official TFR scaled by births reported so far this year versus the same months of last year, the method used by trackers like Birth Gauge and More Births. Hover a country to see its source.

Model

Projections are a standard cohort-component model run in your browser: single-year ages 0–100+, annual steps from mid-2026 to 2100. Each year people age one year and die according to the UN's projected life tables, births come from the chosen TFR applied to the UN's projected age pattern of childbearing, and migrants are added by age and sex following the UN's projected migration. With the default fertility path it reproduces the UN medium variant to within about 1% for most countries by 2100.

Caveats

Projections are scenarios, not forecasts. Migration is held to the UN's assumptions (scaled by the slider) and doesn't respond to population pressure. Current-year estimates use partial-year births and can move as more months are published. Regions use the UN's regional aggregates. Very small territories can deviate more.
